# A guide to find Product-Market Fit, from someone who has to find it again, to help me and others looking for PMF

*I will continuously edit this until we find PMF.*

*First published: 2026-09-03 | Last updated: 2026-09-03 | Also on [Substack](https://alexfranchtapia.substack.com/p/ce5dd7a3-05e8-4c3d-b9e0-25062baacd25)*

## About me

I’ve been running startups for 7 years. With our first product, we hit PMF, it took a lot of hard work and raw volume, but we hit $500k ARR, and ARR grew for 16 months at 10%+ MoM. We closed over 100 SME & Mid-market customers with our GDPR Compliance Platform called Privasee.

![Privasee growth](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dE6T!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F31158baf-efa1-4e6f-ae12-16e7dca49118_1456x1048.webp)

Then our main acquisition channel, an AI SDR, before those existed, collapsed when AI SDRs started being created as products, Google tightened email spam rules, and people started ignoring their inboxes.

I typically say we arrived at level 2 PMF according to [Levels of PMF from First Round](https://www.firstround.com/levels)

We’re now building [Glassray](https://www.glassray.ai), and actively working towards PMF.

**I’m writing this guide as a way to structure my own thoughts and give another perspective to the sea of other founders who are in the same boat as me.**

My job is building a VC-backed startup, and my hobby is learning about startups and engineering; thus, I consider myself a Startup Athlete. Like any athlete, you win by training, competing, and winning enough that other great people recognise you. I’m on that path today.

> Be cautious of my advice, I found PMF but an early sign of it, this does not qualify me to give advice, but it may help you to learn about my reflections.

## Recommended Reading

The phrase “you will know when you feel it” is bad advice; therefore, others with a big platform and insight into many successful and failed companies have written about this.

1. **[Levels of PMF by First Round](https://www.firstround.com/levels)** ([.md version](https://github.com/afrancht/finding-pmf/blob/main/first-round-levels-of-pmf.md)) is great, as it breaks down PMF into 4 different stages and gives you building blocks:
   1. Levels → **how much PMF you have**
   2. Dimensions → **how to measure PMF in different ways at different stages**
   3. Levers to get unstuck → **how you can go at PMF in a different way**

   ![Levels of PMF](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!snF_!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdc81e173-4da1-4ede-ab30-a92fd5be6985_3840x2095.webp)

2. **[The Arc Product-Market Fit Framework](https://sequoiacap.com/article/pmf-framework)** (.md version) a bit more generic but is great because:
   1. It explains the different **types of companies you may be building**. VC push you towards hair-on-fire problems as those are the easiest to back, but you may want to build something different.
   2. It proposes some **terrifying questions** to critically analyse your company for example: **how is my product 10x better**?
   3. Explains the **Path to Conviction** you must have to get PMF, starting from your internal vision & belief and up to, will people pay enough to build a business.

   [![The Arc Product-Market Fit Framework (video)](https://img.youtube.com/vi/18A84qjVbeo/0.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18A84qjVbeo)

3. **[The Foundation Sprint](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/introducing-the-foundation-sprint)** by John Zeratsky and Jake Knapp (creators of the design sprint). The podcast version gives you a framework to get started and start by validating your assumptions in an agressive way, while looking at your problem to solve from many directions.

   [![The Foundation Sprint (podcast)](https://img.youtube.com/vi/UbjAOCzpNWc/0.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbjAOCzpNWc)

## Learnings from my experience

1. Choosing **your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) is the most important thing**, as humans drive purchase decisions. Before PMF, you will need relationships to get feedback and sell ([further reading](https://posthog.com/newsletter/ideal-customer-profile-framework)).
2. **The easiest path is for first-time founders to build hair-on-fire companies.** Exiting your company makes it much easier to build a future vision company. I see this in my network and Elon Musk is a good example; he’s building SpaceX now but started with PayPal. As a first-time founder, it’s confusing as VCs ask you to think big and the examples you know are SpaceX etc. For a VC, think big means there’s a big market, **not necessarily** that it should change the universe/world.
3. **Timebox your PMF finding.** One of the moments of most progress for the company in a time of uncertainty was running our YC without YC experiment. It timeboxed experiments into 2-week sprints with clear decision criteria at the end, and it showed exactly why YC is so great for founders.
